# Sources for reporting principles

These sources inform the `paper` skill. They do not establish one template for every discipline; select guidance by study type, purpose, and publication venue.

1. International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (ICMJE). [Preparing a Manuscript for Submission to a Medical Journal](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/manuscript-preparation/preparing-for-submission.html). Guidance on research article structure, study-design reporting, citations, and results; primarily intended for biomedical publishing.
2. EQUATOR Network. [Reporting guidelines](https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/). A library of health research reporting guidance by study type, including CONSORT, STROBE, PRISMA, and related guidelines.
3. Altman, D. G., & Simera, I. (2010). [Responsible reporting of health research studies: transparent, complete, accurate and timely](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2793689/). *BMC Medicine, 8*, 24. Discusses transparent, complete, and accurate reporting of health research.
4. National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine. (2017). [Communicating Science Effectively: A Research Agenda](https://doi.org/10.17226/23674). National Academies Press. Synthesizes research on communicating science in relation to goals/audiences and uncertainty; it cautions against treating knowledge deficits as the only explanation.

## Application

- ICMJE and EQUATOR focus on health research; do not generalize field-specific requirements into universal standards.
- Select a guideline for the study type and venue; check its original checklist before claiming compliance.
- Principles broadly useful across report types are: provide enough source/method detail for evaluation, avoid overstating results, state limitations/uncertainty, and fit the writing to its purpose and audience.
- “Vietnamese public-facing report” is an editorial form for general readers, not the name of an official national standard.
