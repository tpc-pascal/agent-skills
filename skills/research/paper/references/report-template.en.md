# Vietnamese public-facing report

This form explains a topic or set of findings to general readers in clear Vietnamese. It is an editorial choice, not a formal academic standard. Adapt the structure to the purpose; no heading is mandatory in every report.

```markdown
# <Title that accurately names the topic or main conclusion>

## Summary
<The question, main finding, and significance; usually 1–3 short paragraphs.>

## Main text
<Only the sections needed to answer the question. Explain terms at first use.>

## What is known and what remains uncertain
<Evidence, confidence, limitations, and open disagreements. Omit if already clear above.>

## Conclusion
<A conclusion proportionate to the evidence; do not add unsupported recommendations.>

## References
<Only sources cited in the report; include page/section when available.>
```

## Editorial rules

- Lead with what readers need to know; use short sentences, plain language, and sourced examples.
- Separate evidence, interpretation, and uncertainty. Avoid sensationalism and do not turn correlation into causation.
- Keep tables, figures, metadata, and technical detail only when they aid understanding. Put raw data, full extractions, transcripts, or tool logs in linked sidecars.
- Preserve enough citation detail for readers to check claims. Do not prefix every sentence with an evidence label; use the shared labels in `output-format.en.md` when distinctions need clarification.
- Vietnamese is the default; add a translation only when requested or needed by the audience.
